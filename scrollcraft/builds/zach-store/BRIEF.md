# Zach Store: brief

Interviewed 2026-10-20 (four questions in one pass). Where a topic was not asked,
the answer below is an **authored decision**, marked as such, made inside the
direction the owner chose.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe.** Owner chose: *"Dark premium-minimal."* Authored reading of it for this
   brand: a dark showroom after hours, one red sun (the logo's), everything
   labelled like a museum object. References (authored): a camera shop's glass
   counter at night; a Muji price card; a museum wall label.
2. **Scroll journey.** Authored from the existing site's own sections: the shop's
   flagship laptop first, then the rule the shop runs on, then the whole range,
   then the flagship's numbers, then business orders, then the questions people
   ask before they pay and where to find the shop.
3. **Energy curve.** Authored: calm open, very quiet second act, the loudest
   moment in the middle (the collection), steady after, calm close.
4. **Feeling and the one moment.** Authored, see the curve and peak below.
5. **One thing no other site does.** Authored from the shop's own value on the
   about page ("Tell us your budget. We'll show you the best device for it, not
   the most expensive one"): the visitor sets a budget on a price ruler and every
   product says how much money they would keep.
6. **Distance from premium-minimal.** Owner chose premium-minimal itself.
7. **One world or distinct scenes.** Owner chose: *"Distinct chapters."*
8. **Assets.** Owner's existing files: the logo, and three product posters
   (MUSONY W1, Redmi Note 13, HP 15s). No footage. No generation key on this
   machine, so nothing is generated: the posters are cropped and cleaned of their
   baked-in marketing text, and products without photos get drawn plates.

Other answers from the interview:

- **Shop facts:** *"Placeholders for now."* Address, phone, hours, email and GST
  number appear as clearly marked `[TBC]` slots. Nothing is invented. The SEO data
  only states facts the site already states.
- **Demo state:** *"Keep demo mode."* Banner and "nothing is sent" notes stay, quieter.

## Who it is for and what they must believe

A shopper in Chennai (or a small office) comparing phones and laptops, wary of
online shops that hide the real price. By the end they must believe:
**this shop shows the whole price and would rather you spend less.**
The one action: **Browse the collection** (used as the label everywhere).

## Grammar

**Gallery / catalog.** The real question of a shop visitor is "what are the
options and what do they cost", which is exactly this grammar's fit. Labels carry
fact, not pitch, which is also the brand's voice ("Fewer promises. Better numbers").

## Feeling curve

| Act | Feeling | What causes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Object one | Confidence | The flagship laptop already on the wall with its full price on a label, the red sun behind it, planes separating as you scroll |
| 2 The rule | Intimacy, quiet | One index card, small type, a lot of dark around it (the authored silence before the peak) |
| 3 The collection | Delight, then relief | Eight objects in a row, priced low to high; you drag the price ruler and what is over budget steps back while the rest tell you what you keep |
| 4 The numbers | Weight | The W1 photo holds still while its four real figures land one at a time |
| 5 For teams | Practical assurance | The ground turns from dark to bone; the estimate answers the number of units you type |
| 6 Questions and the visit | Resolve | Plain answers to what people ask before paying, then the shop's address plate, and the page stops there |

**Authored silence:** act 2 is deliberately short on content and long on space. It
is not dead scroll.

## The peak

> "I put my budget in and every laptop told me how much money I'd have left over."

Lives in act 3. It has the largest scroll span on the page and the only bespoke
interaction.

**It's the site where** you drag a ruler to your budget and the shop shows you
what you would keep instead of what you could spend.

## Signature move

**The budget ruler.** A logarithmic price ruler ($10 to $2,000) under the
collection. Every product is a tick at its real price; clicking a tick walks the
rail to that object. Dragging the budget handle regrades the whole collection:
objects over budget recede (smaller, greyer, quieter) and say "Over by $X";
the ones that fit say "Leaves you $Y". A "you are here" bracket on the ruler
follows the rail as it pans. The budget is remembered and carried into the cart
drawer ("Cart is $Z under your budget").

## Score

| Act | Device | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Object one | `parallax` (four planes) + `tilt` on the plate | Depth from separate planes: light, sun, photo plate, label |
| 2 The rule | `reveal` (up wipe) on the card | The card being uncovered is a change of state |
| 3 Collection | `pan` + bespoke ruler | Lateral travel reads as range; prices rise left to right like the ruler |
| 4 The numbers | `pin` + `count` | The photo holds, real figures land |
| 5 For teams | `reveal` (ground wipe to bone) + live estimate | Ordering for a team is a different mode, so the ground changes |
| 6 Close | `flow` + `in` | Plain reading; the address plate holds as the last screen |

Families: parallax, tilt, reveal, pan, pin, count, flow. No family twice in a
row. No `scrub` (no footage, and none needed). Grammar bans respected: no
kinetic headlines, no spotlight, no magnet.
